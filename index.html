Finding 1

Same MT topics, different meanings

Communities discuss overlapping topics such as translation quality, efficiency, and reliability, but frame them through different priorities.

👨‍💻

AI developers

Performance, model behavior, compute cost, robustness, hallucination.

🧑‍🎓

Language learners

Usability, dialect/language coverage, explanations, learning support.

🏢

LSPs

Client-facing workflow, privacy, business value, adoption.

🌐

Professional translators

Quality nuance, post-editing burden, rates, control, oversight.

Finding 2

Conflict grows within communities

The paper operationalises within-community conflict as sentiment dispersion multiplied by the coexistence of positive and negative views.

C = σs × 4p+p−
dispersion × polarity mixing
σs
standard deviation of sentiment scores
p+
share of positive posts
p−
share of negative posts
Interpretation: high conflict means a topic contains both strong sentiment spread and mixed positive/negative views inside the same community.
Finding 3

Sentiment is not simply AI vs. users

1
LSPs are most positive overall.MT is often framed as useful infrastructure for language-service workflows.
2
Professional translators are most critical.Concerns cluster around quality, cost, trust, and labour impact.
3
Non-developer communities are heterogeneous.Learners, LSPs, and translators do not form one unified user perspective.

Method

From public posts to community-level findings

A transparent pipeline for collecting, filtering, annotating, validating, and analysing MT discourse.

Step 1

Collect

Public MT-related posts/comments from four free-access platforms.

Step 2

Filter

Remove spam, advertisements, and off-topic material.

Step 3

Annotate

Community, sentiment, subject, aspect, verb–object claim.

Step 4

Validate

Human evaluation for key automatic annotations.

Step 5

Analyse

Within-community conflict, cross-community sentiment, framing gaps.

For MT evaluation

Go beyond benchmark accuracy. Include trust, workflow burden, labour impact, and practical oversight.

For system design

Different stakeholder groups need different interfaces, assurances, and control mechanisms.

For community research

Cross-community analysis can reveal conflicts that single-community studies miss.
